## 2. Why the PASS appears real

### CASE-01 — Weak incumbent / cross-location attention

Lane C correctly overturns the KPI-dashboard default using the supplied session and escalation data.

Its additional value over the controls is the deeper framing:

> the operating-axis conflict is known-location/reporting work versus cross-location exception work.

It also preserves unrelated domains instead of pretending the evidence supports a total IA redesign.

**Result:** C materially beats A and B.

### CASE-02 — Sound incumbent / dense reconciliation table

All three lanes preserve the table.

This is useful evidence because PAR does **not** manufacture differentiation or recommend novelty merely to look intelligent. Lane C treats the table as a comparison workbench and preserves the incumbent.

The judge did not call C's advantage over A/B material.

**Result:** correct restraint; no artificial win.

### CASE-03 — Landing / competing commercial intents

Lane C identifies three consequential decisions rather than issuing a CRO checklist:

1. CTA hierarchy;
2. mechanism/proof sequencing;
3. shared page vs distinct enterprise path.

Crucially, it does not claim the final shared-vs-segmented architecture is already known. It explicitly asks what evidence would flip the decision.

Both A and B were judged to overcommit to detailed architecture in an intentionally underdetermined case.

**Result:** C materially beats A and B.

### CASE-04 — Early product / decision-flip question

This is the strongest proof case in Wave 01.

Lane C's entire first turn is essentially one question:

> Does the returning operator normally know the client, or first discover urgent work across the portfolio?

Only after the response establishes that roughly three quarters of recurring sessions start cross-client does C select work-first.

Baseline A and comparator B both select work-first **before obtaining that evidence**. Comparator B additionally states an unprovided behavioral claim about how agency staff “typically” start.

**Result:** C materially beats A and B; direct demonstration of the core product thesis.

### CASE-05 — Insufficient public evidence

Lane C refuses the founder's request to manufacture an authenticated IA from a marketing taxonomy.

It reframes the question to the dominant returning-work axis and labels its model as provisional rather than implementation guidance.

Baseline A recommends a concrete authenticated structure and receives a fatal false-certainty failure. Comparator B is substantially better, but still weakly privileges a work-queue default.

**Result:** C materially beats A and B; strong public proof of useful refusal.

### CASE-06 — Reframe / Team context

Lane C recognizes that “Should Teams move into the sidebar?” is downstream.

The product contains two different jobs:

- active-Team context/switching;
- Team administration.

C correctly promotes the semantic distinction and separates high confidence in the reframe from lower confidence in the exact navigation pattern.

**Result:** C materially beats B; C vs A is not counted as a material win on both core axes.

## 4. Important limitations

Wave 01 validates a **specific capability thesis**, not universal UX superiority.

### L1 — The rubric is PAR-shaped

The cases and scoring contract intentionally test:
- consequential decision decomposition;
- decision-flip questioning;
- evidence sufficiency;
- refusal;
- reframe;
- adversarial convergence.

That is appropriate for falsifying PAR's stated thesis, but it makes the raw `99.38/100` Lane C mean unsuitable as a general-purpose “UX quality score.”

**Do not lead publicly with 99.38/100.**

### L2 — Same model family for runner and judge

The runs and blind adjudication were performed in the same broader model environment. Blindness removes lane identity, but it does not create a fully independent human or cross-model evaluator.

This is a reason to phrase claims as “in our frozen blind bake-off,” not “objectively better.”

### L3 — Controlled synthetic cases

The six cases are intentionally constructed to isolate decision behavior. They are valuable methodology tests, but they are not evidence of real-world adoption, user value, or market demand.

Real public product reviews should be the next evidence source.

### L4 — Comparator mapping is strong but finite

Lane B uses pre-frozen, case-matched public skills, which is materially stronger than comparing only against one generic skill. It still does not prove superiority to every product/UX method or every possible configuration.

### Defensible

> In a frozen 6-case blind hostile bake-off, Product Architecture Review V0 materially outperformed the preselected case-matched public comparator on 5 of 6 cases.

> PAR scored 99.38%, therefore it is 99% accurate.

## 6. Best public proof cases

### #1 — CASE-04: One answer flips the IA

Best launch example.

The baseline/comparator decide “work-first” from the concept description. PAR asks one question first, receives the decisive operating-session distribution, and only then commits.

**Public lesson:** Better product judgement is often not another recommendation; it is knowing the one fact that would make the recommendation legitimate.

### #2 — CASE-05: Refusing fake certainty

Best epistemic-discipline example.

The founder asks for authenticated navigation from marketing pages. PAR refuses the false premise while still producing useful candidate models and the minimum evidence required.

**Public lesson:** An AI product expert should sometimes make fewer decisions, not more.

### #3 — CASE-03: Separating direction from unresolved architecture

Strong landing-page example.

PAR can say the CTA hierarchy and information sequence should change while refusing to pretend it already knows whether the enterprise journey deserves a dedicated page.

**Public lesson:** “There is enough evidence to change this” and “there is enough evidence to freeze the final architecture” are different statements.

### #4 — CASE-02: Defending the boring incumbent

Good anti-novelty example.

The dense table survives because the workflow is comparison-heavy, desktop-heavy, and bulk-action-heavy.

**Public lesson:** Product architecture review is not redesign theater.

### #5 — CASE-06: Reframe before placement

Strong architecture example.

“Should Teams move out of Settings?” becomes:
“How should active workspace context differ from workspace administration?”

**Public lesson:** Many navigation questions are object-model/context questions in disguise.

Packaging/editorial changes are safe if they do not alter runtime behavior:

- README;
- installation instructions;
- examples;
- provenance;
- benchmark report;
- public explanation of statuses;
- repository organization;
- launch copy.

If `SKILL.md` or behavioral reference logic changes, call it a new candidate/version.
